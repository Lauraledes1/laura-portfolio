An Escalation Ladder for AI in the B100 Operations Portal

B100's Ops team rebuilds every payment case by hand across three disconnected systems. I designed a three-level escalation ladder, ambient, copilot, agentic, with the transitions and trust patterns that hold it together while money is moving.

Timeline

2026, in design

Role

Lead Designer, Conversational & Agentic AI

Method

Escalation-ladder framework + interaction-pattern specs + one end-to-end flow

Team

1 Designer (Design & AI architecture) + 1 Engineer

Context & Opportunity

To explain why a payment is stuck, an Ops analyst rebuilds the case by hand across three disconnected systems, the card processor, the provider that moves the money, and a legacy database linking them, with no single screen that shows all three.

It's agent-shaped work: multi-source retrieval and reasoning. But money is moving, so confidence, citations, approval gates and an audit trail have to be built in from the start.

The framework

The Escalation Ladder

Three levels of AI along one continuum. As you climb, four things move together: who takes initiative, whether the AI can change state, how much trust the interaction demands, and how reversible its output is. What makes it a ladder rather than three separate products is the transitions, how an analyst moves up and down without breaking context.

Five patterns

Confidence signal

One vocabulary across levels, confirmed / likely / uncertain, shown on hover at L1, inline at L2, in the audit trail at L3. Never a bare number floating on its own.

Citation & sourcing

Every agent claim is tied to its source, Gen 3 / Transfermate / Gen 2, with the time it was queried. Hover at L1, inline at L2, in the audit trail at L3.

Permission model

Actions are typed. Safe (retrieval, drafts) needs no approval. Reversible (label a transaction, save a filter) needs a simple approval. Destructive (retry settlement, send external comms) needs approval with details.

Graceful degradation

If confidence drops at L3, the agent proposes L2, "I'm not sure, want me to investigate?" If L2 can't answer, it proposes a human handoff rather than guessing.

Escape hatch

At any level, any time, the analyst can get to "just show me the raw data." The agent is never the only door to the information or the action.

Where AI doesn't go

Direct Ops data entry

Mapping a new buyer, for example, not automated until there's enough volume to justify the calibration cost.

Compliance & legal approvals

Human, non-negotiable. The agent can assemble the packet; it cannot sign off.

Terminal states of money already moved

Audit yes, action no. Once funds have settled, the interaction is read-only by design.

image
image

Trade-offs & open questions

Three levels, not two or five

Two collapses ambient into copilot and loses the "the product got smarter" move. Five splits hairs Ops won't feel. Three maps to the real thresholds: does it talk back, and can it change state.

Batch approval for destructive actions

Far faster for the 12-transaction case, but one "approve all" click carries more weight. Mitigated with per-item opt-out and undo, still open whether high-value transactions should force per-item approval.

Open, whose name is on the action?

Accountability blurs between the human who approved and the agent that executed. The audit trail captures the full chain, but the org still has to decide where responsibility sits.
